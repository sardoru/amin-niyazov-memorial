# Amin Irmatovich Niyazov — Content Reference

## Key Biographical Facts
- **Full name:** Amin Irmatovich Niyazov (Амин Ирматович Ниязов / Amin Ermatovich Niyozov)
- **Born:** 7 November 1903, Ak-Tepe (Актепа), Kasan district, Kashkadarya Oblast (per Russian Wikipedia; English says Fergana Oblast)
- **Died:** 26 December 1973 (aged 70), Tashkent
- **Father:** Farmer/sharecropper (издольщик) who worked for wealthy landowners
- **Nationality:** Uzbek
- **Party:** Communist Party (joined 1925)
- **Education:** All-Union Industrial Academy "J.V. Stalin" (graduated 1934)

## Career Timeline
1. **1919:** Started working — Fergana district nutritional/food committee, then secretary of Fergana city Komsomol committee
2. **1920s:** Worked in Turkestan Cheka and GPU (secret police/security), then Head of Finance Department of Fergana Oblast
3. **1926:** Appointed head of financial department of Fergana Oblast Soviet executive committee
4. **1929:** Headed the state seed fund
5. **1930-1934:** Studied at All-Union Industrial Academy "J.V. Stalin" in Moscow
6. **1934:** Sent to construction of Chirchik electrochemical combine — started as foreman, then site manager, then chief engineer
7. **1935+:** Various leadership roles in business and party
8. **1940-1946:** People's Commissar (Narkom) of Finance of the Uzbek SSR — **THIS WAS DURING WWII**
9. **1946-1947:** Deputy Chairman of the Council of People's Commissars (later Council of Ministers) of Uzbek SSR
10. **1947-1950:** Chairman of the Presidium of the Supreme Soviet of the Uzbek SSR (= head of state of Uzbekistan)
    - **Key achievement:** Initiated construction of major irrigation facilities — Kattakurgan and Chimkurgan reservoirs
    - Land development in Central Fergana and Hungry Steppe (Голодная степь)
    - Improved irrigation in Bukhara, Samarkand, and Khorezm oblasts
11. **April 1950 - December 1955:** **8th First Secretary of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of Uzbekistan** (= de facto leader of Uzbekistan)
    - Predecessor: Usman Yusupov (who became USSR Minister of Cotton)
    - Successor: Nuritdin Mukhitdinov
12. **December 22, 1955:** Dismissed after visit by Bulganin and Khrushchev. Blamed for failing to increase cotton production.
13. **1955-1956:** Minister of Municipal Economy of the Uzbek SSR (demotion)
14. **1956-1957:** Continued as Minister

## National-Level Positions
- **October 1952 - February 1956:** Member of the Central Committee of the CPSU (Communist Party of the Soviet Union)
- **1946-1958:** Deputy of the Supreme Soviet of the USSR

## Awards
- Two Orders of Lenin
- Three Orders of the Red Banner of Labour
- Two Orders of the Red Star

## WWII Connection (from Babadzhanov memorial context)
- During WWII (1941-45), both grandfathers — Gulam (Bobojonov, the silkworm/parachute scientist) and Amin (Niyazov, as People's Commissar of Finance) — were in the "highest strategic echelon" of the country's war effort
- Amin managed the entire financial system of Uzbek SSR during wartime, when Uzbekistan became a major evacuation destination for Soviet industry and people

## Political Context
- Part of the "Fergana group/clan" (ферганская группировка) in Uzbek politics
- His dismissal came during Khrushchev's post-Stalin power consolidation
- Was succeeded by Sharaf Rashidov as Chairman of Supreme Soviet (who later became the famous/infamous long-serving First Secretary)
- The cotton production politics that led to his dismissal foreshadowed the much larger "Uzbek Cotton Affair" of the 1970s-80s

## Historical Significance
- Rose from sharecropper's son to leader of one of the largest Soviet republics
- Oversaw Uzbekistan during critical post-war reconstruction period
- Initiated massive irrigation infrastructure that transformed the agricultural landscape
- One of the few Uzbek leaders of the Stalin era who survived the purges
- Chirchik electrochemical combine he helped build became one of the largest industrial facilities in Central Asia

## Family Connection
- Great-grandfather of the Umarov family (mother's father's father)
- His granddaughter married into the Umarov family
- Both he and Gulam Bobojonov (maternal great-grandfather from mother's mother's side) served at the highest levels during WWII
